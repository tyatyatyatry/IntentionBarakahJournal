Welcome to your Barakah Journal

A quiet place to begin your day with an intention, end it by looking back, and notice where Barakah shows up in between.

Why this exists

“Actions are only by intentions.” (Bukhari & Muslim)

Barakah isn't about doing more in less time. It is getting better results with the time you have, through sincere intention, connection with Allah, and mindful action. This is not a to-do list. Nothing here needs ticking off. It is here to help you notice, and to nudge you gently.

How to use it

  1. Morning: state an intention. One sentence about what you intend today, and for whom.
  2. A short guided pause. After you write, the journal walks you through six screens, one idea each. It reads what you are doing and matches it to one of eleven postures of the heart (Ikhlas, Tawakkul, Dhikr, Shukr, Sabr, Taqwa, Silaturrahim, Rahmah, Sadaqah, Ihsan, Amanah). It asks you one question about why, reads your answer, then offers a new way to see it, a short story, and the Qur'an or hadith underneath. At the end you check in with your heart and set the intention, with your own words if you want to change them. If the match is off, tap Not quite? Choose another lens; the journal remembers. Where it can, a small free model on your device reads the meaning of what you wrote. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  3. Night: look back. Choose which intention you are reflecting on, or the day in general, and write freely. Afterwards you get “What's next”: ideas for tomorrow's intention.
  4. Watch your four trees grow. Date palm for Faith & Worship, olive for Character & Heart, pomegranate for People & Society, fig for Life & Sustenance. Each posture belongs to one tree. An intention gives its tree 1 drop; looking back on it gives 2 more.

Up to 7 intentions and 7 reflections a day, so it stays light. Your words stay on this device only; use Backup (top right) to keep a copy.
